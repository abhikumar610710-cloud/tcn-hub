1 What is Coinbase Wallet?

Coinbase Wallet is a self-custody wallet: the wallet's private keys are controlled by the user rather than held for them in a custodial exchange account. It can be used to manage supported crypto assets and NFTs and connect to onchain applications. You do not need a Coinbase.com exchange account to use a self-custody wallet.

Do not confuse Coinbase Wallet with a regular Coinbase exchange account. In the current product transition, Coinbase calls its mobile onchain experience Base App, while its downloads page continues to identify the browser extension as Coinbase Wallet. Always follow the official download page for the current app and extension options.

💡

Quick Fact

Self-custody means you control access to your wallet. It also means you are responsible for protecting recovery details and checking every address, network and transaction.

⚠️ Important network-support update — 15 August 2026

Coinbase says its Classic Wallet closed on 15 August 2026 and lists several older networks as unsupported. Sending assets on an unsupported network can result in loss. Before moving funds, check the current official network list and Coinbase's migration instructions. Supported networks also vary by wallet type.

6 Security Tips

  • 🛡️Never share your recovery phrase. Coinbase says it will never ask for it. Never paste it into a website or chat.
  • 🔎Verify the official app. Open Coinbase's download page yourself and avoid links sent by strangers.
  • 🧾Review token approvals. Check which assets a DApp wants permission to access before approving.
  • 📋Read transaction previews. Treat them as estimates and independently verify the network, recipient and transaction.
  • ⚠️Check network compatibility. Sending to an unsupported network can result in loss; confirm the wallet type and receiving network first.
