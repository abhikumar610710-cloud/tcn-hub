Coinbase Wallet

🔵 1. Overview

Coinbase Wallet is a self-custody (non-custodial) wallet where you control your wallet keys or recovery credentials rather than keeping funds inside a custodial exchange account. It lets you manage supported digital assets and NFTs and interact with onchain applications without requiring a Coinbase.com exchange account.

Do not confuse Coinbase Wallet with a regular Coinbase exchange account. In Coinbase's product lineup, the mobile wallet experience is branded as Base App, while the browser extension continues to be listed as Coinbase Wallet. Always check Coinbase's official download page for current app and extension details.

✅ What It Helps You Do

Manage supported tokens and collectibles, connect to onchain apps, view estimated transaction previews, and control your wallet access directly.

⚠️ What to Keep in Mind

Self-custody means full responsibility for backups, network checks, and approvals—and supported networks vary by wallet type and recent platform updates.

⚠️ Network-Support Update (15 August 2026): Coinbase states that its Classic Wallet closed on 15 August 2026 and lists several older networks as unsupported in the current wallet. Sending funds on an unsupported network can result in loss—always verify current supported networks and migration guidance before moving assets.

⭐ 2. Key Features

Coinbase Wallet offers core features for self-custody asset management and onchain interaction:

🔎 Transaction Preview & Approval Alerts

Displays estimated token balance changes and token-approval details before confirming supported dApp transactions (noted by Coinbase as supported on Ethereum and Polygon; previews are estimates, not a safety guarantee).

🚫 dApp Blocklist & Permission Control

Can display warnings for applications flagged as potentially malicious and lets you review and manage connected dApp permissions.

🖼️ NFT & Multi-Network Asset Support

Supports holding and viewing supported tokens and NFTs across compatible networks depending on your selected wallet type.

🔒 Hardware Wallet Support

The browser extension supports connecting hardware signers such as Ledger for additional signing protection.

💸 3. Fee

Understanding fee components before transacting helps avoid failed transfers or unexpected costs:

Wallet Setup & Viewing Downloading and creating or viewing a self-custody wallet is free; you do not pay a recurring fee just to hold a wallet.
Network / Gas Fees Onchain transfers, token approvals, and contract actions require network gas paid in the active chain's native token (such as ETH on Ethereum or Base).
Swaps, Bridges & dApp Fees Any built-in trade/swap quotes, bridges, or third-party dApps may involve network gas, spread/slippage, or protocol fees—always check the exact quote before confirming.
Unsupported Network Risk Sending funds on an unsupported network or wrong chain can lead to permanent loss or extra recovery complexity—always verify compatibility first.

🌐 4. Network Support

Network availability depends on which type of wallet you create or use. Do not assume every wallet type supports every chain:

Smart Wallet (Passkey) Supports EVM-compatible networks; does not support Bitcoin or Solana.
Email / Social Login Wallet Supports EVM-compatible networks, Solana, and Bitcoin (Bitcoin uses a separate address from the primary EVM address).
Mnemonic Wallet Supports EVM-compatible networks, Solana, and Bitcoin; back up the recovery phrase securely for each wallet.
Preconfigured Networks & Legacy Note Examples include Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, Avalanche C-Chain, BNB Chain, Monad, Robinhood Chain, and Zora (plus Bitcoin/Solana where supported by wallet type). Always check official docs for current support and post-15 August 2026 network changes.

🗝️ 5. Custody & Control

Coinbase Wallet is a self-custody wallet, meaning you control your wallet access rather than a custodial exchange holding your keys:

📜 Recovery Phrase & Backup Access

Your recovery phrase or backup credentials control access to your self-custody wallet. Keep backups private and offline where required.

🚫 No Exchange-Style Recovery by Support

Coinbase does not have access to your self-custody recovery phrase and cannot reverse blockchain transfers once confirmed.

🛡️ 7. Security

Protect your funds by combining official downloads and careful checks with disciplined habits:

Use a Secondary Wallet Use a secondary wallet with limited funds when testing new dApps, claims, or unfamiliar links so your main wallet stays isolated.
Never Share Recovery Phrase Never share your recovery phrase or keys with anyone—including anyone claiming to be support. Never type it into a website or chat.
Check Previews & Token Approvals Review token approvals and treat transaction previews as estimates rather than a guarantee; verify network and recipient details independently.
Confirm Network Compatibility Verify both wallet type and supported network before sending or bridging assets so funds are not sent to unsupported chains.

❓ 10. FAQ

1. Is Coinbase Wallet the same as a Coinbase exchange account?

What it is: No—Coinbase Wallet is a self-custody wallet, whereas a regular Coinbase.com account is a custodial exchange account with a different custody model.

How to use it: Keep in mind how assets are held in each and follow official download and security steps when using the wallet.

2. Do I need a Coinbase.com account to use Coinbase Wallet?

What it is: No—Coinbase's help documentation notes that you do not need a Coinbase.com account to use a self-custody wallet.

How to use it: Start from coinbase.com/wallet/downloads and set up your chosen wallet type directly.

3. Is Coinbase Wallet now called Base App?

What it is: Coinbase's current downloads page brands its mobile experience as Base App while listing the browser extension as Coinbase Wallet.

How to use it: Check the official downloads page to confirm current app and extension options before installing.

4. Which networks are supported across different wallet types?

What is supported: Smart wallets (passkey) support EVM-compatible networks (not Bitcoin or Solana), while email/social login and mnemonic wallets also support Bitcoin and Solana per Coinbase's current network guide.

How to check: Verify your wallet type and check the official supported network list before sending or receiving funds.

5. What happened on 15 August 2026 regarding network support?

What happened: Coinbase states that its Classic Wallet closed on 15 August 2026 and lists several older networks as unsupported in the current wallet.

How to handle it: Check Coinbase's official migration guidance and current supported network list before attempting transfers on older networks.

6. What fees apply when using Coinbase Wallet?

What fees apply: Wallet setup is free, while onchain actions require network gas fees and any swaps, bridges, or dApp actions may involve gas, spread/slippage, or protocol charges.

How to check: Review the fee, network, and output details shown before confirming a transaction.

7. Why should I use a secondary wallet and check token approvals?

What it is: A secondary wallet keeps limited funds separate for testing new dApps or links, and token approvals control what a smart contract can access.

How to stay safe: Use a secondary wallet on unfamiliar sites, check token approval prompts carefully, and review/revoke unused permissions.

8. Can Coinbase support recover my recovery phrase or reverse a transfer?

What to know: Coinbase states it does not have access to your self-custody recovery phrase, and confirmed blockchain transfers cannot simply be undone.

How to stay safe: Never share your recovery phrase with anyone claiming to be support and always double-check network and address details first.
